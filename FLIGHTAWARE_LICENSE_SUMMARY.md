# FlightAware AeroAPI Premium License Rules

Here is a detailed breakdown of each rule from the license agreement, simplified for our team.

## What We CAN Do

**Using a single authorized account for multiple users at the same time.**
The license allows for concurrency. This means you do not need to purchase a separate paid account or API key for every developer, data scientist, or team member on your staff. They can all operate under your main organizational account.
* One account works for your entire team.
* Everyone can log in and work at the same time.
* You save money by not having to buy extra individual logins.

**Searching, downloading, and retrieving flight data using their proprietary API.**
This outlines the primary function of the service. You are allowed to programmatically request and pull specific flight information from their databases, provided you are strictly using FlightAware's official API tools to do so.
* You can ask the system for specific flight information.
* You can download the answers it gives you.
* You must use their official tool (the API) to do this.

**Creating "Derivative Works" based on their original data.**
You are allowed to take their raw numbers and process them through your own formulas, algorithms, or logic to create brand new insights, calculations, or metrics that do not exist in the original data.
* You can use their data as an ingredient to make something completely new.
* If you calculate a new number using their information, you own that new number.
* This new creation is what they call a "Derivative Work."

**Using the data internally for application development, marketing, customer support, and analytics.**
Your company can use this data entirely behind the scenes. Whether you are building prototypes, analyzing market trends, running marketing campaigns, or helping a customer track a package, internal operational use is fully permitted as long as the raw data isn't exposed publicly.
* Your team can use the data behind the scenes to run your business.
* It is a great tool for testing new apps or helping your customers.
* You can use the numbers to figure out business trends and analytics.

**Embedding the data or Derivative Works into applications for consumers (B2C) or businesses (B2B).**
Once you integrate the data into your software, you have the green light to sell or distribute that software. This applies whether you are making an app store flight tracker for everyday people (B2C) or a complex management tool for airport administrators (B2B).
* You can put the data inside apps you build for regular people.
* You can put the data inside software you build for other companies.
* The data must be built into the app's interface, not just given away as a raw file.

**Sharing the data with third parties if embedded into your product or altered.**
You are allowed to pass the information on to outside users, but there is a strict condition: the data must be baked into your software interface or transformed into a new metric (a Derivative Work). You cannot simply hand over a spreadsheet of their raw database.
* You can let other people see the data through your app or website.
* You can freely share the new insights and math you calculated.
* You cannot just hand a copy of the raw data files to someone else.

**Using the data for academic research with proper attribution.**
Students, professors, and researchers can use this information in published papers or studies. The conditions are that the data must be summarized or processed (not raw), and FlightAware must get clear, written credit as the source in the publication.
* You can use this information for school or university research.
* You must clearly give credit to FlightAware in your paper.
* You cannot publish the raw data numbers directly in your research.

## What We CANNOT Do

**Selling, renting, or sublicensing your API access or the raw data.**
FlightAware strictly prohibits you from acting as a middleman. You cannot resell their raw data as a standalone product, and you cannot let another company "rent" your access keys to bypass paying FlightAware for their own account.
* You cannot sell your login details to anyone else.
* You cannot rent out your data access.
* You cannot become a middleman selling their raw data to others.

**Storing raw data for more than 30 days vs. storing Derivative Works.**
FlightAware limits how long you can keep their exact, original data in your own databases to a maximum of 30 days. However, any new metrics or insights (Derivative Works) you calculated from that data can be kept permanently.
* You must delete the exact data they give you within one month.
* You can keep anything new you created from that data forever.
* This rule stops you from building a massive, permanent copy of their database.

**Creating situational displays for commercial aircraft.**
The data provided through this API is not certified or intended for critical live-navigation or safety-of-life displays. It cannot be used in commercial airplane cockpits or official air traffic control radar screens.
* Pilots cannot use this data to fly planes.
* You cannot use this to build a radar screen for commercial flights.
* It is not meant for real-time safety or navigation.

**Helping passengers substantiate claims against airlines.**
You cannot build a service or use the data to help people sue airlines or claim financial compensation for things like delayed or canceled flights under passenger rights laws (like EU 261).
* You cannot use this to help people sue airlines.
* You cannot use it to help passengers get refund money for delays.
* The data is absolutely not meant for legal fights.

**Using web scrapers, collection agents, or bots.**
You must fetch the data exactly how FlightAware designed the API to be used. You are strictly prohibited from writing scripts that scrape their public website or use bots to bypass their official API gateway.
* You have to use the front door (their official tool) to get the data.
* You cannot use bots to secretly copy data from their website.
* No sneaky data collection is allowed.

**Reverse engineering or decompiling the API.**
You are strictly forbidden from trying to take apart FlightAware's software to see how it works under the hood. You cannot attempt to copy their source code, break their security, or steal their trade secrets.
* You cannot try to hack or break apart their software.
* You cannot try to steal their secret code.
* You just use the tool; you do not take it apart.

**Using the API as a backup for another data provider.**
If you use a competitor of FlightAware as your main data source, you cannot just use FlightAware to fill in the gaps when the competitor goes down or misses a flight, unless you get explicit written consent from FlightAware first.
* You cannot use FlightAware just as a backup for a different company.
* You cannot mix their data with a competitor's data to fill in missing spots.
* You need special written permission to do either of these things.

**Allowing airlines to use FlightAware Foresight data.**
Commercial airlines are strictly barred from using FlightAware's specific "Foresight" predictive data endpoints, or anything you build using that data. This is a zero-tolerance rule, and breaking it instantly cancels your contract.
* Airlines are absolutely not allowed to use the "Foresight" prediction data.
* Airlines cannot even use the new things you build from that prediction data.
* If you break this rule, your account gets canceled immediately.
